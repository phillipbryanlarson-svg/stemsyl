English uses the spelling of each word you finished from the strip or typed on a keyboard. A word spelled only by sound comes out as its sounds between slashes. Pasting English into this keyboard brings back the Stemsyl, and a backup file keeps everything.

Consonants — colour is the family, up · centred · down

Up voiceless · centred the family’s sonorant · down voiced. The families run round the colour wheel from the tongue tip to the back of the mouth, so a misread lands on a related sound.

Vowels — colour is the vowel, centred · up · down

The vowels take six rainbow colours plus grey, two each: red a · eye, orange ah · ow, yellow e · ay, green i · ee, blue u · oo, grey uh · aw, violet oh · oy. Centred is the plain vowel, up glides to i, down glides to u.

How the keys work

Every key is one sound. The note groups the sounds of a word into syllables by itself: each vowel takes the consonant just before it on its left and the one just after it on its right. Any other consonant stands on a short stub of its own.

After the sounds of a word, the strip above the keys offers the English words that sound like it, and longer words that begin with it. Tapping one finishes the word and keeps its spelling for Copy English; the note itself shows only the sounds.

The ◆ key is the name mark: it goes before a name, where English uses a capital letter. Numerals are dice on a 3 × 3 grid — count the lit squares.

Typing on a physical keyboard

Type English. Each word turns into its sounds when you press space, Enter or a punctuation key. A capital letter in the middle of a sentence adds the name mark. Digits type numerals. Arrows move the cursor; ⌘/Ctrl-Z undoes.
